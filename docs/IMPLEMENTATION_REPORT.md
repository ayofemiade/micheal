## 2. How the hero works

- **Server-rendered first frame.** CSS container units lay the photographs out at the exact 1:1 camera, so the first paint is the finished composition. This was verified with JavaScript disabled, and the result is pixel-identical to the JS frame.
- **One camera, two transforms.** `scene.ts` computes the camera: log-scale zoom, with panning anchored by interpolating the visible width. `PullBackMotion` applies one transform to the photographs and one to the drawing. Nothing else moves.
- **Resolution.** A high-resolution face crop (an exact crop of the print, generated by the image script) covers 1:1 and fades out as the full print becomes sharp enough.
- **Composition by shape.** Wide stages get the side-by-side composition. Phones *and portrait tablets* get the stacked one, with a white veil keeping copy legible over the shirt.
- **Accessibility.**
  - All four chapters are in the DOM in reading order.
  - Focusing a control in a hidden chapter moves the camera there instantly (verified: visible within 60 ms).
  - The ruler is a `nav` with `aria-current`.
  - Reduced motion makes the camera *cut* between distances.

## 9. Post-launch fix: "some parts are not loading well"

**Report.** After the site went live (micheal-ebon.vercel.app), Segun said some parts weren't loading, and scrolling straight after arrival showed an unloaded scene.

**Causes (measured on the live site and with a throttled browser):**

1. **On-demand image optimisation.** Every image size and format was a separate Vercel request taking 0.5–1.5 s, and cold variants were encoded to AVIF on the spot (`x-vercel-cache: MISS`). A first visit asked for dozens of them.
2. **The hero camera waited for GSAP,** which loaded at browser idle. Scrolling before then did nothing.
3. **Hero prints defaulted to lazy loading.** They start off-screen, so they began loading mid-zoom.
4. **The room drawing hid itself while GSAP loaded,** leaving a blank figure on slow connections.

**Fixes:**

- **Static images.** `scripts/optimize-images.mjs` now pre-builds WebP at 480/800/1200/1600/2400 px, and a custom loader (`src/lib/image-loader.ts`) serves them as plain CDN files. Nothing is encoded on request.
- **Camera on native scroll.** `PullBackMotion` uses a rAF scrub with no library, so it's live as soon as the page hydrates. GSAP is now only used below the fold.
- **Everything loads up front.** All homepage images are `loading="eager"`, and the below-the-fold ones are `fetchPriority="low"`. After any scroll on a throttled connection, 24 of 24 images were loaded.
- **Animations never hide content while waiting.** `DrawOn` and `LineReveal` only hide their content once the animation can run, and not at all if it's already on screen.
- **The arrival** (`components/home/Arrival.tsx`): a studio-white screen that blocks scrolling until fonts and all 11 hero images are decoded, then lifts.
  - Capped at 3 s from navigation start, with a CSS-only failsafe at the same time.
  - Skipped on repeat visits in the session; never shown without JS; holding pages unaffected (lock scoped with `:has()`).
  - Measured: lifts at **0.5 s** on a fast connection and **3.0 s** on throttled slow-4G, with everything decoded.

**Cost.** Page weight rose from 565 KB to about 1.2 MB, because all photographs now load up front. Lighthouse mobile: 83–91 (LCP 2.9–3.9 s simulated, TBT 240 ms). Desktop: 99.

**Needs a redeploy** to reach the live site.

## 9b. Post-launch fix: "it jumps straight to Stories"

**Report.** On Segun's laptop, scrolling from the hero did nothing for about five wheel notches, then cut to Stories. It worked on ours.

**Cause, reproduced on the live site.** His computer asks websites to reduce motion. Windows does this when Settings → Accessibility → Visual effects → *Animation effects* is off. The camera honoured that by rounding progress to whole distances, so wheel notches 1–4 changed nothing and notch 5 cut. With the normal setting, every notch zooms. Measured on the live site at 1536×730 with 100 px notches:

| Notch | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| :-- | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Normal: photo scale | 0.76 | 0.57 | 0.43 | 0.33 | 0.24 |
| Reduced (before): photo scale | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | **0.10 (cut)** |

**Fix.** With reduced motion the camera still never travels (the zoom is the kind of motion that setting exists to stop). Instead, each distance now dissolves out and the next dissolves in, eased, so every notch visibly does something. The blue at 1:∞ fades in after the dissolve rather than wiping.

**Phone fixes made at the same time:**

- **Address bar.** The stage was `100svh`, so a strip of page showed under it when the address bar slid away. It is now `100lvh`, and the copy is lifted by `100lvh − 100svh`. Camera progress is measured against the stage height, not `innerHeight`, which changes as the bar moves.
- **Rendering.** Before, one huge photo layer was promoted and demoted at every pause, forcing re-rasterisation. Now every photograph is a permanent layer of its own. The decorative box-shadow is removed so the browser can composite each image directly.
- **Ground grid.** Its layer measured 80,002 × 30,804 CSS px. It is trimmed to just past the widest frame (the line positions are unchanged), and the drawing is hidden while invisible.
- **Idle writes.** Styles are written only when they change (colour variables, `<html data-tone>`, the header's solid state). Invalidation tracing confirms only the necessary transform and opacity writes remain.
- **Ultrawide (3440 px).** At 1:1 the frame caught the edge of a neighbouring print, hugely magnified. The other prints now fade in as the camera pulls back.

**Limits of this verification.** Headless Edge does not measure GPU rasterisation or memory, so the layer changes are reasoned from how browsers composite, not measured. Main-thread time was already about 1 ms per frame at phone speed. The address-bar behaviour cannot be emulated on desktop. A real-phone check is still needed.

## 9d. Load speed: every photograph once

**Finding.** On phones, every MAO frame and the kitchen photo downloaded twice. The hero print and the section copy used different `sizes`, so the browser picked a different file for each. The hero took `mao-blue-800` and the Stories wall took `mao-blue-1200`, and the same split happened for all ten photographs. 100%-zoom desktops had the same split.

**Fix.** One shared `SIZES` constant in `src/data/home.ts` is used by both places, so the second use is served from memory.

**Measured.** Local production build, cache disabled. Slow 4G is 1.6 Mbps with 150 ms latency, plus 4× CPU slowdown on the phone. Each figure is the median of 3 runs.

| | Before | After |
| :-- | --: | --: |
| Phone 3x: image files / weight | 27 / 1,811 KB | 17 / 1,295 KB |
| Phone 2x: image files / weight | 27 / 961 KB | 17 / 715 KB |
| Laptop 1536 px: image weight | 1,026 KB | 992 KB |
| Phone, slow 4G: portrait painted (LCP) | 2.3 s | 2.4 s |
| Phone, slow 4G: every image in | 10.6 s | 8.1 s |

The hero prints now finish about 1.8 s later on a slow phone. They download at the size the Stories wall needs instead of as an extra small copy first.

**Tried and dropped.** I tested a strict queue: the portrait first, then the hero prints, then the sections. It was slower: the arrival lifted at 6.8 s instead of 4.7 s. The browser already fetches the portrait first, because of the preload and `fetchPriority`, and the queue only removed parallel downloading.

**Hosting checked.** The HTML and images are served from Vercel's edge cache (images cached for 1 week, fonts for 1 year). The page responds in about 0.46 s from London. A first visit after the edge cache has gone cold is slower, and that cannot be changed from the code.

## 9g. iPhone crash on the way to Places

**Symptom.** On an iPhone, scrolling toward Places replaced the site with the browser's "Can't open this page" screen. That is the browser ending a page that ran out of memory.

**Cause.** The survey grid and ground line were world-sized SVG geometry sharing one SVG with the house. While the drawing was visible the browser requested a 32,302 × 29,002 px layer, plus an 80,002 px wide layer for the ground line at 1:∞. The 2026-11-06 Places work made the drawing visible earlier and larger, which exposed it. This was measured in Chromium with iPhone dimensions.

| 390 × 844 at 3x, layer memory requested | Before | After |
| :-- | -: | -: |
| Stories (1:10) | 603 MB | 603 MB |
| Drawing on the way to Places | 32,914 MB | 283–400 MB |
| Places (1:100) | 309 MB | 309 MB |
| Possibilities (1:∞) | 35,364 MB | 305 MB |

These are requested sizes: browsers allocate large layers in tiles, so they show which layer was the problem, not exact memory in use.

**Fix.** Nothing on the stage may be sized in world units.
- The grid is one screen-sized CSS layer (`.pullback-ground`).
- The drawing's SVG is exactly stage-sized, and the camera sets its `viewBox` rather than scaling it.
- The photograph no longer keeps `will-change: clip-path`, and the edge blend is hidden once it has faded.

**Rule for future work.** Any new stage element must be bounded by the screen. Check with a layer-memory measurement at 390 × 844 at 3x before shipping.

**Limit of this verification.** The peak across screens measured was 718 MB (430 × 932 at 3x). This has not yet been run on a physical iPhone.
