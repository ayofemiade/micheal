# Media & Communication: reference analysis

Both sites were rendered in a real browser at 1440 × 900 and scrolled from top to bottom, because their text alone says almost nothing about them. Neither was copied. This records what each teaches and what was deliberately left behind.

## She So Co., Our Work

[shesocorporate.com/work](https://www.shesocorporate.com/work)

**What it does.** A black page, a heavy title and two sentences, then 12 images in three tight columns of varying heights. Text labels are minimal. The page ends on a giant wordmark and one call to action.

**Why it works.**
- The introduction is very short, so the work is on screen almost immediately.
- Tight gaps make the images read as one body of energy, not as separate items.
- Varied heights stop the columns reading as a grid.

**What was adapted.** The Coca-Cola project is a dense wall on near-black with small gaps, so it reads as a campaign rather than as separate pictures.

**What was left behind.** The neon colour, the heavy display fonts and the attitude. They suit an events brand, not a personal site about attention. Its project labels are also so thin they would not meet this brief's caption requirement.

## TY Bello, Photography

[tybello.com/photography](https://tybello.com/photography)

**What it does.** A centred name, three navigation links, the word "Photography", then a paged three-column gallery of portrait frames on white, with no captions.

**Why it works.**
- The interface almost disappears, so the portraits carry the identity.
- Saturated, heavily art-directed colour is the signature.
- It shows a photographer's identity can be stated through images alone.

**What was adapted.** The confidence to let a portrait be the whole statement, as on the cover, and to keep captions out of the way in the portrait run.

**What was left behind.** Identical tiles and rounded corners, which this brief rules out. Every frame there is cropped to one shape; here each photograph keeps its own proportions.

## Principles carried into the page

1. **The work arrives first.** One sentence of setup at most before a large image.
2. **Vary the scale.** Full-bleed, large, small and dense, never one size.
3. **Each project gets its own ground.** Different work needs a different room.
4. **The interface steps back.** Captions are small, factual and few.
5. **Nothing needs scrolling to reveal it.** A decision from the Listening Hub: quality comes from composition.
